## 6-Step Mechanical Execution Pipeline

### Step 01: Initial Assessment (Phase 1: Audit)
Equipment schedule review, historical service logs, warranty baseline, and mapping facility heat-load expectations against original design criteria.
- **Deliverable**: Equip. Registry Sheet

### Step 02: Site Inspection & Telemetry (Phase 1: Field Run)
Physical plant audit including precision amp draws, refrigerant pressure diagnostics, laser shaft alignment, and rotating equipment vibration analysis.
- **Testing**: Vibration & Amp Signatures

### Step 03: Technical Proposal (Phase 2: Estimation)
Itemized scope of work with transparent OEM equipment specs, calculated component lead times, and locked-in guaranteed fixed pricing without change orders.
- **Commercial Terms**: Firm Fixed Bid

### Step 04: Scheduling & Logistics (Phase 2: Ops Plan)
Coordinated crane pick permits, off-peak rigging windows, and comprehensive safety plans formulated specifically to prevent any tenant disruption.
- **Coordination**: Rigging & Traffic Logistics

### Step 05: Installation & Service (Phase 3: Build & Tune)
OSHA-30 and EPA Universal certified mechanical technicians executing exact piping, electrical terminations, and refrigerant recovery per ASME standards.
- **Standards**: EPA Universal · OSHA 30

### Step 06: Verification & Digital Turnover (Phase 3: Closeout)
Certified air and hydronic balancing (TAB), BACnet control sensor calibration, baseline benchmark reports, and full digital maintenance binder turnover.
- **Final As-Built**: NEBB Certified TAB Record

## Pre-Maintenance Intake: Facility Readiness Checklist
*7 key facility checkpoints to streamline technician dispatch:*

1. **Building Address & Access Logistics**: Confirm exact roof hatch locations, service freight elevator dimensions, keycard protocols, and security escort desk contact details. *(Access Required)*
2. **Equipment Brand & Model Numbers**: Clear photograph or transcript of compressor/chiller nameplate stamps, model designations, and serial tag codes. *(Critical ID)*
3. **System Architecture Designation**: Specify plant topology: Packaged RTUs, Split DX, Water-Cooled Centrifugal Chiller, Hydronic Gas Boilers, or Open Cooling Towers. *(Plant Class)*
4. **Last Service Date & Historical Logs**: Recent oil analysis reports, last chemical water treatment records, filter/belt change logs, and any major compressor overhauls. *(Baseline Records)*
5. **Description of Existing Symptoms**: Specific active alarm codes, abnormal harmonics/vibrations, differential pressure drops across coils, or static air pressure imbalances. *(Telemetry Data)*
6. **Site Safety & Environmental Clearance**: Facility-specific safety orientations, site badging protocols, lock-out/tag-out rules, and ARC flash PPE requirements. *(Compliance)*
7. **Preferred Low-Impact Service Windows**: Tenant acoustic restrictions, off-peak utility demand windows, evening crane approvals, or designated weekend testing slots. *(Schedule)*

## Frequently Asked Technical Questions (FAQ)
*REV 2024.3 // ASHRAE 90.1*

1. **How do you service equipment in fully occupied commercial or medical buildings?**
   > We isolate mechanical service work behind engineered containment barriers when necessary, utilize low-decibel vacuum and recovery equipment, and schedule all high-noise procedures (such as crane rigging or high-pressure flushing) during pre-approved off-peak quiet hours. Our technicians carry badging credentials and maintain cleanroom and occupied-space protocols.
2. **What is the standard recommended maintenance frequency for central plants?**
   > Commercial central chiller and boiler plants typically require bi-monthly runtime verification, belt and bearing inspections, quarterly filter changes, and seasonal water treatment audits to sustain efficiency and maintain warranty protection.
3. **What is the emergency dispatch protocol if our plant experiences an unexpected trip?**
   > Contract holders have direct radio lines into our master mechanic roster, guaranteeing 2-to-4 hour emergency response times across Indiana, Ohio, and Northern Kentucky.
4. **What measurable energy efficiency recovery is achieved through regular coil cleaning?**
   > Dirty heat exchangers and improper refrigerant charges force compressors and fans to draw 10%–20% excess electrical consumption. Our precision sensor calibration, dynamic VFD balancing, and micro-channel coil washes routinely recover full thermodynamic COP ratings within 30 days.
5. **Which commercial and industrial HVAC equipment manufacturers are supported?**
   > We are factory-authorized and warranty-certified across Trane, Carrier, York, Daikin, Lennox, AAON, and Mitsubishi Electric VRF architectures.
